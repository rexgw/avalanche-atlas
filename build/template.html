What the map shows

Each 30 m cell gets a relative risk index from 0 to 100. It combines how likely an avalanche is to start there or reach there (terrain), how unstable the snowpack is (conditions), and how bad the outcome would be (consequence). It is a comparison tool, not a probability.

Terrain layers (pre-computed)

  • Elevation: USGS 3DEP 1 arc-second DEM, resampled to a 30 m grid.
  • Release areas: fuzzy slope membership peaking near 40° (about 0.5 at 29° and 51°), reduced on very rough terrain and below treeline.
  • Runout exposure: energy-line propagation downhill from release areas using a 24° travel angle above treeline and 28° below (forest friction proxy). This catches flat ground under big slopes.
  • Terrain traps: gullies (concave curvature), cliffs (>50°), and abrupt flats below steep slopes.
  • ATES class: simplified AutoATES-style rules: Simple, Challenging, Complex, Extreme from slope angle, release areas, runout overlap and cliffs.

Conditions model

  • Danger rating for each elevation band (BTL < 1500 m, TL 1500–1900 m, ALP > 1900 m) scales hazard roughly ×2 per level, following the Avalanche Canada scale.
  • Avalanche problems raise hazard on the listed aspects and bands (likelihood and size) and lower it elsewhere. Persistent and deep persistent problems extend runout weight. Loose problems shorten it. Cornices add hazard on crests.
  • Weather: new snow loads all aspects above the freezing level. Wind over 15 km/h loads lee aspects near ridge crests. Warming above 0 °C adds hazard on sunny aspects and below the freezing level.

Formula

terrain = 1 − (1 − release) × (1 − 0.85 × runout)
raw = terrain × danger × problem × weather × (1 + 0.6 × trap)
index = 100 × (1 − e^(−1.4 × raw))

Classes: Low < 14, Moderate 14–31, Considerable 31–55, High 55–78, Extreme ≥ 78.

Route scoring

Routes are sampled every 15 m. Route score = 0.55 × 90th percentile + 0.45 × maximum, so one serious crux can't be averaged away by long safe sections. Cruxes are continuous stretches at Considerable or above.

Safest route

A* search over the 30 m grid. Step cost = distance × slope effort + uphill gain + slider weight × distance × (index/100)². Slopes over 48° are treated as impassable. The search finds the path with the lowest total cost.

Limitations

  • No forest-cover data: treeline effects come from elevation bands only, so cleared ski runs and open avalanche paths below treeline are under-weighted.
  • The ski area is not modelled as controlled terrain.
  • Spearhead and Musical Bumps lines are approximate (real waypoints joined by a least-effort terrain path). The Wedgemount line is a real GPS track (Peakbagger, Seán Caulfield) of the summer trail.
  • 30 m cells miss small features like rolls, cornice edges and short gullies.
  • Live data only loads when the page runs outside this viewer (self-hosted or opened as a file).
