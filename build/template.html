Tactile 3D print

Download an STL of the mountain to print and explore by touch. Routes stand up as smooth, rounded ridges. Each risk class has its own sharp-edged pattern so it never feels like the natural terrain: Low is smooth, Moderate has diagonal ribs, Considerable has round flat-topped studs, High has square pyramids and Extreme has tall, tightly packed spikes. The texture follows the conditions set in the Conditions tab at the moment you download. Preview print shows the exact model on the map before you download it.

A touch legend runs along the south edge. From west to east it has five texture patches from Low to Extreme, marked with one to five raised dots, then a smooth ridge showing how a route feels.

Heights are exaggerated 1.5 times on a 3 mm base. Route ridges are 2.2 mm wide and 2.2 mm tall, above the tallest texture. Print with a 0.4 mm nozzle at 0.12 to 0.16 mm layers so the texture survives.

What the map shows

Each 30 m cell gets a relative risk index from 0 to 100. It combines how likely an avalanche is to start there or reach there (terrain), how unstable the snowpack is (conditions), and how bad the outcome would be (consequence). It is a comparison tool, not a probability.

The map is a 3D model of the elevation data, drawn as a 60 m mesh with no vertical exaggeration. The risk or ATES layer, 100 m contours and hillshade are draped over it at 15 m detail.

Terrain layers (pre-computed)

  • Elevation: USGS 3DEP 1 arc-second DEM, resampled to a 30 m grid.
  • Release areas: fuzzy slope membership peaking near 40° (about 0.5 at 29° and 51°), reduced on very rough terrain and below treeline.
  • Runout exposure: energy-line propagation downhill from release areas using a 24° travel angle above treeline and 28° below (forest friction proxy). This catches flat ground under big slopes.
  • Terrain traps: gullies (concave curvature), cliffs (>50°), and abrupt flats below steep slopes.
  • ATES class: simplified AutoATES-style rules: Simple, Challenging, Complex, Extreme from slope angle, release areas, runout overlap and cliffs.

Conditions model

  • Danger rating for each elevation band (BTL < 1500 m, TL 1500–1900 m, ALP > 1900 m) scales hazard roughly ×2 per level, following the Avalanche Canada scale.
  • Avalanche problems raise hazard on the listed aspects and bands (likelihood and size) and lower it elsewhere. Persistent and deep persistent problems extend runout weight. Loose problems shorten it. Cornices add hazard on crests.
  • Weather: new snow loads all aspects above the freezing level. Wind over 15 km/h loads lee aspects near ridge crests. Warming above 0 °C adds hazard on sunny aspects and below the freezing level.

Formula

terrain = 1 − (1 − release) × (1 − 0.85 × runout)
raw = terrain × danger × problem × weather × (1 + 0.6 × trap)
index = 100 × (1 − e^(−1.4 × raw))

Classes: Low < 14, Moderate 14–31, Considerable 31–55, High 55–78, Extreme ≥ 78.

3D print

The STL is built in the browser from the same elevation data at about 0.4 mm spacing, with heights exaggerated 1.5 times. The risk class, from the index smoothed over 150 m, sets a geometric texture with hard edges: diagonal ribs 0.6 mm tall for Moderate, flat-topped studs 0.9 mm tall on a 3.2 mm grid for Considerable, square pyramids 1.2 mm tall for High and staggered spikes 1.5 mm tall on a 2.4 mm grid for Extreme. Low is left smooth. Routes are smooth rounded ridges 2.2 mm tall, so a finger can follow them through the textured ground. The model is watertight with a flat base. A legend strip along the south edge holds one patch for each risk class, textured with that class's pattern and marked with one to five dots, plus a sample route ridge.

Route scoring

Routes and ski runs are sampled every 15 m. Ski areas drawn as shapes (bowls, glades) are scored over every cell inside the shape. Route score = 0.55 × 90th percentile + 0.45 × maximum, so one serious crux can't be averaged away by long safe sections. Cruxes are continuous stretches at Considerable or above.

Safest route

A* search over the 30 m grid. Step cost = distance × slope effort + uphill gain + slider weight × distance × (index/100)². Slopes over 48° are treated as impassable. The search finds the path with the lowest total cost.

Limitations

  • No forest-cover data: treeline effects come from elevation bands only, so cleared ski runs and open avalanche paths below treeline are under-weighted.
  • The ski area is not modelled as controlled terrain. Ski runs are scored as if uncontrolled, and patrol work, closures and grooming are not included.
  • Ski runs, lifts and uphill routes come from OpenStreetMap. Some runs are split or slightly off their real line, and about 900 m of the Blackcomb uphill route is filled with a least-effort path where the trace has a gap.
  • Spearhead and Musical Bumps lines are approximate (real waypoints joined by a least-effort terrain path). The Wedgemount line is a real GPS track (Peakbagger, Seán Caulfield) of the summer trail.
  • 30 m cells miss small features like rolls, cornice edges and short gullies.
  • Live data only loads when the page runs outside this viewer (self-hosted or opened as a file).
